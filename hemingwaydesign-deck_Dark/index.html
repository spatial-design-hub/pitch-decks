01 / Your challenge

Success brings people.
Places have to be ready.

Your festivals and placemaking projects draw tens of thousands of visitors. The more a place succeeds, the more its streets, squares and arrival routes have to carry.

Three questions every destination faces

  • Where will people walk, on an ordinary day and on the big day?
  • Which streets and spaces will feel crowded, and where are the pinch points?
  • How will visitors arrive, and what will extra trips add to stations, stops and roads?
In your words

“A ‘test and learn’ pilot, informing future public realm, transport, and movement decisions.”

HemingwayDesign on Centre Stage, Richmond. Our tools can give that learning a movement evidence base, before, during and after an event.

Quotation from HemingwayDesign’s published article on narrative-driven placemaking festivals.

03 / Connectivity tool · Capability

See which streets
hold a place together

Our connectivity tool scores every street by how well it links the wider network, revealing the routes people are most likely to rely on.

Why it matters to a destination

The strongest links are where arrival spaces, wayfinding and entrances work hardest. The weakest show where a new connection could open up a place.

Test before you change

Close a street for a festival or add a new route, then rerun the analysis to see how movement could shift.

Capability demonstration on a city-centre street network. Connectivity describes network structure, not observed footfall.

04 / Pedestrian demand tool · Capability

See where people will walk,
every day and on the big day

Our pedestrian demand tool sends walking trips from arrival points, such as stations, car parks and hotels, to destinations, such as venues and squares, and maps the busiest routes.

Scenario by scenario

Weight origins and destinations for an ordinary day, an event day or a new attraction, then compare the results.

Down to pedestrian comfort

Where demand concentrates, we compare flows with the footway space available to grade comfort and show where people would feel squeezed, in line with Pedestrian Comfort Level (PCL) principles.

Capability demonstration on a city-centre network with venue destinations. Modelled demand is an estimate to support design decisions; it does not replace detailed crowd safety assessment.

05 / Multimodal Travel Platform · Capability

Know how people get there,
and what it adds to the network

Our Multimodal Travel Platform compares door-to-door journeys by car, rail, bus, park and ride, walking and cycling, from every origin to a chosen destination.

Built for workplace moves

It shows how relocating a workplace changes every employee’s journey, with travel-time catchments, route comparisons and active-travel potential.

Ready for visitors

The same approach can map where a destination’s visitors come from, how they are likely to travel and which stations, stops and routes would carry the extra trips.

Platform demonstration from a workplace relocation study, with client branding removed. Applying it to visitor travel is a proposed use.
